(c) + (f) merged: Monotonicity of entailment, and incremental-SAT clause survival

Prior art. The §3 argument is sound in its core and it has a name: it’s the monotonicity of classical entailment — if F ⊨ C then F ∪ G ⊨ C — combined with the soundness of resolution (learned clauses are logical consequences of the formula, per Robinson’s 1965 resolution paper; any CDCL correctness proof, e.g. the GRASP paper by Marques-Silva & Sakallah, IEEE Trans. Computers 1999, rests on it). It is standard enough that the incremental-SAT literature treats it as given rather than proving it. The lineage of systems that exploit it:

  • Hooker, “Solving the incremental satisfiability problem,” Journal of Logic Programming 15(1–2):177–186 Springer (1993) — the earliest treatment of preserving solver work when a clause set is incremented with a new clause, i.e. exactly the pure-addition direction.

  • Whittemore, Kim & Sakallah, “SATIRE: A New Incremental Satisfiability Engine,” DAC 2001 ResearchGate — the first CDCL-era solver handling both addition and removal; its key mechanism is tracking which learned clauses depend on removed clauses so only those are discarded. This is structurally identical to Rodin’s support sets, ten years avant la lettre.

  • Eén & Sörensson, “Temporal Induction by Incremental SAT Solving,” ENTCS 89(4), 2003 Semantic Scholar — introduced the now-dominant assumption/selector-literal technique: clause deletion is simulated by guarding clause groups with fresh literals; learned clauses automatically inherit the selectors of everything used in their derivation, so “deleting” a group disables exactly the dependent lemmas, with no unsoundness and no dependency bookkeeping beyond the literals themselves.

  • Later refinements: preprocessing soundness in incremental settings (which shows the failure mode explicitly: a resolvent can survive erasure of its parent clause and wrongly imply UNSAT of a now-satisfiable formula); AAAI Nadel, Ryvchin & Strichman, “Ultimately Incremental SAT,” SAT 2014; and IPASIR, the standard incremental-solver interface (Balyo et al., 2016). Springer

  • Outside SAT solvers proper, the strongest analog to “reuse an UNSAT verdict under an entailment-monotone edit” is the symbolic-execution caching line: Green (Visser, Geldenhuys & Dwyer, FSE 2012 — persistent cross-run reuse of constraint results after canonicalization) Springer and especially GreenTrie (Jia, Ghezzi & Ying, ISSTA 2015, arXiv:1501.07174), which checks implication relationships between constraints and returns without invoking the solver when the queried formula implies a cached unsatisfiable formula, ResearchGate plus Recal/Recal+ (Aquino et al., ISSTA 2015, “Reusing constraint proofs in program analysis”). ResearchGate GreenTrie is your §3 argument, deployed in production research tooling: new formula entails old formula ⇒ old UNSAT verdict transfers.

  • No production package resolver does this across manifest edits, as far as I could find. Resolvo advertises “incremental/lazy solving,” GitHub but that means lazy metadata fetching within one solve, not fact survival across formula edits. PubGrub reuses incompatibilities only within a single solve. This appears to be genuinely unoccupied territory — stated plainly, not padded.

Strongest candidate technique for Rodin. Reframe lifting as an entailment check on premises, not a byte-equality check on read-sets. For a fact F with replayable proof DAG D: F lifts across a manifest edit iff every leaf premise of D is entailed by the new constraint set. For Rodin’s premise kinds this is decidable cheaply:

  1. Premise::Clause for edge e with old accepted set R_old: entailed by the new manifest iff edge e still exists and R_new ⊆ R_old. Subset is one VersionSet operation you already have: R_new ∩ complement(R_old) = ∅. This covers req-narrowed (708 instances) and is vacuously satisfied by dep-added (125 required + the optional ones, see caveat below), because pure addition leaves every old premise untouched.

  2. Premise::Exclusion (yank/availability): snapshot-scoped; safe across manifest edits at fixed snapshot_id, never safe across snapshot updates — the WidenCert.snapshot_id field already encodes exactly the right scoping, keep it.

  3. Premise::Pin: safe iff root constraints unchanged or narrowed (same subset test).

  4. Premise::Hypothesis: discharged within the DAG, no external condition.

To answer your specific question — does interval narrowing actually correspond to clause addition, or is the analogy overloaded? It corresponds to clause replacement by a logically stronger clause, which decomposes as {add stronger clause; delete weaker clause}, and the deletion is harmless because the deleted clause remains derivable from the new one (“Y ∈ R_new” ⊨ “Y ∈ R_old” when R_new ⊆ R_old — disjunction introduction, or in region terms, VersionSet weakening). So the proof technique transfers provided Rodin’s proof format admits one new rule: VersionSet weakening (enlarge a set appearing positively in a premise). With that rule, any old proof mechanically replays under the new formula by prefixing each narrowed premise with a weakening step. Without that rule, replay-verification would reject lifted facts even though they’re semantically valid.

Soundness conditions — and the hole in §3. The hole exists and it is exactly where you suspected: closed-world premises. Monotonicity protects consequences of positive clauses. It does not protect any premise of the form “package P@v has no dependency on Z,” “feature F does not exist on P@v,” or “the versions of Y matching req are exactly {y₁…yₖ}.” Those are closed-world statements about the metadata, not clauses; adding a dep, adding a feature-map entry, or publishing a new version can falsify them while only adding things. Since Rodin resolves features as propagated literals, any fact whose derivation forced a feature literal false via non-existence (“requested feature ‘chrono’ doesn’t exist ⇒ conflict”) has a closed-world premise. The precise safe rule: audit every premise kind and every negative feature literal in the support set; entailment-lifting applies only to facts whose leaves are all monotone-safe (positive clauses, snapshot-fixed exclusions, pins). This also resolves the dep-added-optional question from §3: adding an optional dep is monotone-safe for facts without closed-world feature premises — the new gated clauses are pure additions — and unsafe precisely for facts that consumed the old feature map’s completeness. Note the taxonomy already flags the danger zone: 134 feature-map co-occurrences.

Falsifier (from §2 data). The async-graphql optional-integration churn. Take a fact derived at version v: “region R conflicts because enabling feature chrono on async-graphql@v is impossible (no such feature).” At the release where async-graphql adds the chrono optional dep + feature entry (the §2.3 pattern), the manifest edit is purely additive — the naive §3 rule would lift the fact — and the fact is now false. Second falsifier, orthogonal: any availability-enumeration premise lifted across a registry snapshot change (new version of Y published) rather than a manifest edit; the two edit axes must never be conflated in the cert.

(b) Per-edge validity instead of per-row

Prior art. This is a well-trodden idea outside resolvers; the transfer is the novelty, not the mechanism.

  • Incremental computation with traced reads: Adapton (Hammer et al., PLDI 2014) and Salsa (the incremental framework under rust-analyzer) both key derived results by the precise set of inputs read, and both have the same correctness obligation you’re asking about: every read must be traced. In build-systems terms, Rodin’s current ObservedReadSet digest is a “verifying trace” in the taxonomy of Build Systems à la Carte (Mokhov, Mitchell & Peyton Jones, ICFP 2018), and the per-edge proposal is the standard move to finer-grained trace keys (early cutoff at sub-file granularity — cf. compiler dep-files in Buck2/Bazel-style systems).

  • Datalog/IVM: the support set is why-provenance (Green, Karvounarakis & Tannen, “Provenance Semirings,” PODS 2007): a derived fact survives a delta iff its provenance doesn’t mention a changed input. DRed — delete-and-rederive (Gupta, Mumick & Subrahmanian, “Maintaining Views Incrementally,” SIGMOD 1993) — and counting-based view maintenance are the canonical update-propagation algorithms; differential dataflow (McSherry et al., CIDR 2013) and DDlog (Ryzhyk & Budiu, 2019) are the modern engineered forms. So yes: “facts keyed by which inputs they read, not which containers those inputs live in” is thoroughly known in Datalog engines. What Datalog engines don’t have is your entailment refinement — they invalidate on any change to a read input; direction (c) is what upgrades “unchanged” to “changed compatibly.”

Strongest candidate technique. Store facts indexed by (PkgIx, edge-name) pairs from their support sets, with an inverted index from edge to dependent facts. On a version boundary, diff the manifest row edge-by-edge (you already compute this for the taxonomy), and touch only facts subscribed to changed edges. Crucially, absence must be a first-class subscription: a fact whose derivation observed that P@v has no edge named Z (or no feature F) must be indexed under an explicit absence key (P, ¬Z), so that dep-added events probe the absence index. That converts the closed-world hole from a soundness bug into an indexing requirement. The selector-literal formulation from (f) gives an equivalent implementation with less machinery: one selector per (edge, accepted-set-generation); facts carry selector sets; edits flip selectors.

Soundness conditions. Complete read tracing — the exact condition Adapton/Salsa/BSàlC require, and the classic bug you named. Under-recorded support is unsound in one specific way here: Rodin’s replay-verification of proofs is your safety net only if replay itself re-reads through the traced interface. If the derivation code can peek at metadata without the read being recorded into support, both the fact and its replayable proof will silently agree and both be wrong. The engineering consequence: metadata access during derivation must go through a single choke-point API that appends to the support set, enforced by construction (private raw accessors), not convention.

Falsifier (from §2 data). The rename break: async-graphql 1.10.12 → 1.11.0: async-graphql-parser added; graphql-parser removed in one release. A fact supported only by the graphql-parser edge correctly dies (edge removed). But a fact whose derivation routed through an assumption like “the parser dependency is graphql-parser” without reading the rest of the row — e.g., a conflict derived via a feature that now forwards to the renamed edge — is coupled to both edges while its recorded support may name only one. Simultaneous add+remove pairs are the pattern most likely to expose incomplete tracing, and the dataset shows they occur in the wild. Per-crate spread also bounds the win honestly: serde’s fineness is 1.17x — per-edge tracking buys almost nothing where every break is single-edge on a tiny dep list — while the 7.29x aggregate is carried by async-graphql/hyper-shaped crates.

(a) + (e) merged: Requirement-interval algebra, generalized to a validity lattice over edits

Prior art.

  • Range algebra implementations: node-semver ships subset(r1, r2) (“determine if r1 range is entirely contained by r2 range”) Telerik alongside intersects and simplify. GitHub pubgrub-rs’s Range (now the spun-out version-ranges crate) implements union/intersection/complement/containment over half-open interval unions — same algebra as your VersionSet; exact current API names unverified in this session. Python’s packaging.specifiers has containment but no subset operation on specifier sets, to my knowledge (unverified). Critically, I found no resolver in any ecosystem that uses this algebra to skip re-derivation — it’s used to compute intersections during search (PubGrub’s term arithmetic) and for tooling queries, full stop.

  • The general formulation you’re asking about in (e) already exists — it’s the ATMS label (de Kleer, “An Assumption-based TMS,” Artificial Intelligence 28(2), 1986). An ATMS never marks a derived fact valid/invalid; it maintains, per fact, the set of minimal assumption-environments under which the fact holds. “The set of manifest edits under which an incompatibility remains sound” is precisely an ATMS label where the assumptions are per-edge metadata states — a downward-closed set in a product lattice, ordered by (c)’s entailment relation. Abstract interpretation (Cousot & Cousot, POPL 1977) supplies the lattice vocabulary but nothing package-specific. On the resolver-formalization side — EDOS (Mancinelli et al., ASE 2006, which established NP-completeness of package installability and built the dose3 lineage), CUDF/dose3 (Treinen & Zacchiroli), PubGrub (Weizenbaum, 2018), “Dependency Solving Is Still Hard, but We Are Getting Better at It” (Abate, Di Cosmo, Gousios & Zacchiroli, SANER 2020), and Max-SMT resolution (PacSolve, Pinckney et al., ICSE 2023, lightly verified) — none formalizes edit-space validity of learned incompatibilities. Not found ≠ doesn’t exist, but I looked and am saying so plainly.

Strongest candidate technique. Replace WidenEvidence::ObservedReadSet‘s digest with a per-edge bound certificate: for each supporting edge e, store the pair (must-exist, upper-bound VersionSet S_e), meaning “this fact is valid at any version of the parent whose edge e exists with accepted-set ⊆ S_e,” plus absence keys for closed-world premises. Widening a fact across versions v₁…vₙ then reduces to n·|support| subset tests instead of digest equality, and — the (a) question — “does this requirement edit intersect this fact’s region” is answered by R_new ⊆ S_e, one intersect-with-complement per edge, which is strictly cheaper than re-derivation (no search, no propagation). The lattice view earns its keep in one concrete way: certificates compose. The meet of two facts’ certificates is the certificate of anything derived from both, so Premise::Derived nodes get their certs for free during replay rather than by re-analysis.

Soundness conditions. Everything from (c) (monotone-safe premises only; snapshot-scoped), plus one that’s specific to (a): the interval algebra must implement the ecosystem’s actual matching semantics, not textbook intervals. If from_req and the subset test disagree with semver::VersionReq::matches on any input, certificates are unsound. Your probe-based classification method already acknowledges this; the certificate path must use the same exact semantics.

Falsifier (from §2 data). Prerelease gating. The req-reshaped rows like ^2.0.0-alpha.0 → ^2.0.0-alpha.1 — where caret-on-a-full-prerelease-triple degenerates to near-exact semantics — are exactly where a naive interval reading (treat ^2.0.0-alpha.1 as [2.0.0-alpha.1, 3.0.0)) computes wrong subset relations, because prerelease versions match only under narrow conditions that differ between Cargo and npm (npm’s documented rule: a range like >1.2.3-alpha.3 admits prereleases only on the same [major, minor, patch] tuple; GitHub Cargo’s rule differs in detail). Any certificate produced from a subset test that mishandles this lifts facts across boundaries where the accepted set actually changed. Your 203 req-reshaped instances are the regression suite for this falsifier.

(d) Fact weakening instead of invalidation

Prior art. Split verdict:

  • In TMS literature: yes, essentially solved for booleans. JTMS (Doyle, “A Truth Maintenance System,” Artificial Intelligence 12(3), 1979) handles retraction by re-labeling via justifications; the ATMS (de Kleer 1986) makes retraction a non-event — labels already condition every fact on its assumptions, so “weakening” is just dropping environments from a label. Whether it generalizes past booleans: the label machinery is boolean over assumptions, but nothing prevents the assumptions from being region-valued predicates; the generalization is your (e) certificate, so I’d call it “generalizes, with the region algebra doing the work the ATMS never had to.”

  • In Datalog/IVM: DRed (Gupta et al. 1993) is literally “over-delete, then re-derive what survives” on retraction — the semantics you want, though its recomputation strategy is the expensive version of it.

  • In incremental SAT: no direct prior art found for weakening learned clauses across formula edits. Clause vivification (Piette, Hamadi & Saïs, ECAI 2008) and learned-clause minimization (Luo et al., IJCAI 2017) strengthen clauses with respect to the same formula — different problem. Stated plainly: this direction is where Rodin would be doing something without a template.

Strongest candidate technique. Proof replay with residuals. When edge e’s clause weakens from “Y ∈ R_old” to “Y ∈ R_new” (widening or shift), the old premise is recoverable conditionally: under the added assumption Y ∉ (R_new \ R_old), the new clause yields Y ∈ R_new ∩ ¬(R_new \ R_old) ⊆ R_old. So: replay the proof DAG; at each weakened leaf, conjoin the residual restriction into the fact’s region (region.versions[Y] ∩= complement(R_new \ R_old)); if the region survives non-empty, keep the weakened fact. This handles all three loosening kinds uniformly: req-widened (28 instances — residual excludes the newly admitted versions), req-bumped-incompatibly (709 instances — the shift decomposes as narrow⊗widen; only the widened side contributes a residual; for the dominant ^0.x → ^0.(x+1) pattern the residual excludes the new 0.(x+1) window, which typically doesn’t intersect a fact about old-window conflicts at all, so the fact often survives unweakened in effect), and dep-removed (no residual form exists; the fact dies — weakening cannot save it, and shouldn’t). That last point is the honest boundary: weakening extends coverage to loosening moves on surviving edges only.

Soundness conditions. (i) The residual construction requires each weakened premise to appear positively and be expressible as old = new ∧ restriction — true for VersionSet premises by construction, false for removed edges and for closed-world premises (same exclusion list as (c)). (ii) Replay must be mandatory, not optional: a weakened fact is a new fact and must pass the same replay-verification gate as any learned no-good, or the residual arithmetic becomes an unchecked trust point. (iii) Residuals accumulate multiplicatively across edges within one break; correctness is unaffected but the region can silently shrink to empty, at which point you’ve paid replay cost for nothing.

Falsifier (from §2 data). The lockstep-pinned siblings. async-graphql’s =3.0.0-alpha.3 → =3.0.0 exact-pin bumps make R_new \ R_old the entire useful range; the residual annihilates the fact’s region. With 2.75 edges touched per break on that crate, residuals from multiple simultaneous edges compound and weakened facts collapse to empty almost immediately — correct, but pure overhead. Weakening’s value is therefore inversely correlated with exactly the churn pattern of the crate that motivated this investigation, which is worth knowing before building it.

Ranking (expected soundness ÷ engineering cost)

1. (c)+(f) — entailment-gated lifting with a premise-kind audit. Cleanest soundness story in the whole set (monotonicity is bedrock; the closed-world exclusion is a finite, auditable list over your existing Premise enum), moderate cost (per-edge subset tests + one weakening rule in the proof checker), and it converts the two largest tightening categories — 708 req-narrowed and 125 required dep-adds — from invalidations into no-ops. The selector-literal implementation pattern is twenty years mature.

2. (b) — per-edge support indexing with first-class absence keys. Cheapest mechanism (indexing + diffing you already do for the taxonomy) and the measured 7.29x headroom, but it carries the field’s classic soundness risk (complete read tracing) and its win is unevenly distributed (1.17x on serde). Ranked below (c) on soundness, not on value — and note the two multiply: (b) decides which edges a fact watches, (c) decides which changes on those edges are harmless. Built together they’re one mechanism.

3. (a)+(e) — per-edge VersionSet-bound certificates. Once 1 and 2 exist, this is mostly a data-model refactor of WidenEvidence (bounds instead of digests) plus certificate composition at Derived nodes; the incremental cost is small and it’s the correct final form. On its own, ranked third only because it presupposes the other two.

4. (d) — replay-with-residuals weakening. Largest remaining coverage (the 709 incompatible bumps), no prior-art template in incremental SAT, highest implementation complexity (proof-DAG rewriting under a verification gate), and value that degrades on exact-pin lockstep churn. Build last, after the entailment machinery it depends on exists anyway.
