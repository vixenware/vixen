# Naming committee — seat 1 (Opus 4.8)

Status: independent committee round, 2026-07-08. A codex seat runs in parallel;
these two seats are deliberately uncoordinated. Context read first:
`eu-sovereignty-positioning.md`, `content/provenance.md`.

**Scope of the naming problem.** The *language* is `vix` (the fox) — living,
playful, "it'll always live in my heart," and it is not up for renaming. What
needs a name is the **institutional product**: the registry + build
infrastructure + the compliance/provenance surface — the noun a French ministry
writes into a tender, a CISO defends to a board, and a sovereignty fund
evaluates against a shelf of Gaia-X-shaped disappointments. Two-brand
architecture: `vix` produces, the institutional name *attests*.

---

## The three personas, seriously

**P1 — French public-procurement officer drafting a tender.**
Works in a formal administrative register. The name has to be dictatable over
the phone, spellable without a gloss, and free of false friends in French. It
must read as *infrastructure*, not as a consumer startup — procurement prose
does not want to look like it bought an app. Latinate and metrological/legal
vocabulary travels natively here (the French state's own IT base is literally
called a *socle*; the reference standard is an *étalon*; a supply chain is a
*filière*). Allergens: anglo puns, `-ly`/`-ify` startupisms, anything that
smells of crypto/blockchain (instant credibility death in a public tender).

**P2 — CISO at an EU-critical-sector company under CRA/NIS2.**
Buys *assurance* and has to defend the line item to a board. The name must
signal evidence, seal, provenance, control — and must not collide with an
existing security vendor, or the board deck creates confusion. It has to sound
like an assurance platform, not a developer toy. Gravitas over cleverness: a
CISO cannot stand up and say "we secured the estate with `BuildSnap`."

**P3 — Sovereignty-fund evaluator, post-Gaia-X.**
Carries a specific scar: Gaia-X was a *grandiose, mythological, pan-continental*
name (Earth-goddess, a "sovereign cloud federation") that shipped governance
documents instead of a product. So this persona is now allergic to cosmic
European branding — the `-Stack`, the `-X`, the pantheon. What earns trust is a
name that sounds like it *does one concrete thing well* and is unmistakably
European without being a political umbrella. The counter-position is baked in:
"the product exists and the sovereignty properties are structural." The name
must not undercut that with a promise-shaped word.

**The productive tension:** P1 rewards Latinate administrative gravitas; P3
punishes Latinate *grandiosity*. The sweet spot is Latin/EU vocabulary that is
**clerical and evidentiary** (a seal, a certified copy, a hallmark, a records
office) rather than **mythological and cosmic** (a goddess, a federation, a
stack). Concrete over cosmic. A stamp, not a pantheon.

## (b) Name directions

### 1. **Vidimus** — *"we have seen"* — **96/100**
A *vidimus* (medieval Latin, "we have seen") was an official **certified true
copy**: a sealed attestation that a reproduced document matches the authentic
original. That is precisely what a vixen receipt is — an attested statement of
the observed read-set: *we have seen the entire input graph; this record is
true by construction.*
- **Pronunciation:** VEE-dee-mus — clean in every EU language; Latin travels.
- **Collision:** obscure Latin, no security/build namesake; highly ownable. The
  institutional-tech tradition (Atos, Idemia, Thales, Veritas, Qualys) shows an
  opaque-but-dignified Latin mark is a *feature* for this buyer, not a bug.
- **Anti-Gaia-X:** the opposite register — a dry notarial verb, not a cosmic
  goddess. Humble, procedural, evidentiary. The anti-federation.
- **Two-brand:** `vix` produces; every artifact ships its *vidimus*. Faint
  phonetic kinship (vi-x / vi-dimus) reads as family, not collision.
- Scores: C1 5 · C2 5 · C3 4.5 · C4 5 · C5 5 · C6 4 · C7 4.

### 2. **Poinçon** — the assay hallmark — **87/100**
The *poinçon* is the state-authorised mark stamped into precious metal by an
assay office guaranteeing purity and provenance (the *Poinçon de Paris*). The
semantic fit is almost too good: a build system that stamps a *verifiable mark
of guaranteed provenance into every artifact.* For P1 this resonates in the
bones — it is French state-authority vocabulary.
- **Pronunciation:** pwan-SON — the `ç` and the nasal `-on` are a real, recurring
  tax on German/Italian/Spanish tongues; the pan-EU buyer stumbles.
- **Collision:** clear of cyber/build; some jewellery/watch usage. Ownable.
- **Trade vs. Vidimus:** richer *instant* meaning to a French reader, paid for
  in pronunciation friction across the rest of the Union.
- Scores: C1 5 · C2 5 · C3 2.5 · C4 4.5 · C5 5 · C6 3.5 · C7 5.

### 3. **Tabularium** — the Roman public records office — **83/100**
The *Tabularium* was the state archive on the Capitoline where Rome's
authoritative records were kept. Semantically bullseye — the sovereign,
append-only record of what was built — and it evokes exactly the
transparency-log / receipts story.
- **Pronunciation:** ta-bu-LA-ri-um — clean phonemes but five syllables; heavy
  in a sentence and on a logo.
- **Collision:** essentially none; very ownable.
- **Caveat:** the length works against P1's "dictatable" test and against a
  product that has to fit in a CLI (`tab`? already a data term).
- Scores: C1 4.5 · C2 5 · C3 3 · C4 4.5 · C5 4.5 · C6 2.5 · C7 4.

### 4. **Filigrane** — the watermark — **83/100** *(collision-capped)*
*Filigrane* (FR) / *Filigran* (DE) / *filigrana* (IT, ES, PT) / *filigree* (EN)
is the near-perfect cross-EU **cognate**: the same word, the same meaning —
security watermarking woven into the substrate of banknotes and passports — in
five EU languages. Semantic bullseye for "artifacts that carry their own
recipe": proof embedded in the material, visible when you hold it to the light
(audit). On pure merit it would top the list.
- **Pronunciation:** fee-lee-GRAN(-eh) — genuinely pan-EU; its cognate status is
  a rare, decisive C3 win.
- **Collision — disqualifying for the top slot:** **Filigran SAS** is a *known
  Paris-based cybersecurity vendor* (OpenCTI / OpenBAS). Same country, same CISO
  audience. A P2 board deck would conflate them. This is why a name that scores
  9/10 on meaning cannot be the recommendation — the collision check earns its
  place in the process.
- Scores: C1 4.5 · C2 5 · C3 5 · **C4 1.5** · C5 4.5 · C6 3.5 · C7 4.5.

### 5. **Aval** — the guarantor's endorsement — **78/100**
In French banking-legal register, an *aval* is a formal guarantee/endorsement on
a negotiable instrument (*donner son aval* = to sign off, to back). It also means
*downstream* — the direction provenance flows. A CISO likes "aval" = an
authoritative sign-off.
- **Pronunciation:** ah-VAL — short, clean, pan-EU.
- **Collision:** common word; "Aval" is a bank name (Raiffeisen Bank Aval).
  Low *sector* collision but weak distinctiveness; ownability moderate.
- Scores: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4.5 · C4 3 · C5 4.5 · C6 3 · C7 3.5.

### 6. **Cachet** — the seal — **77/100**
Bilingual EN/FR: a *cachet* is a wax seal / mark of authenticity, and carries a
faint air of prestige. Elegant, short, understood on both sides of the Channel.
- **Pronunciation:** ka-SHAY — travels well.
- **Collision:** some existing usage; the "prestige/cosmetic" connotation
  slightly dilutes the evidentiary read. Ownability moderate.
- Scores: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 4.5 · C4 3 · C5 4 · C6 3 · C7 3.5.

### 7. **Étalon** — the reference standard — **76/100**
*L'étalon* is the authoritative measurement standard held by the state (the
BIPM — keeper of the world's standards — is French; *étalon-or* = the gold
standard). Superb fit for the "cargo is the oracle / ground-truth" positioning.
- **Pronunciation:** ay-ta-LON — the `é` and nasal are a mild tax; and *étalon*
  also means "stallion" in French (a minor, real snicker risk in a tender).
- **Collision:** moderate; ownable-ish.
- Scores: C1 4 · C2 4 · C3 3.5 · C4 3.5 · C5 4.5 · C6 3 · C7 3.5.

### 8. **Socle** — the foundational base — **76/100**
The *safest, most procurement-native option.* The French state's official
free-software base is literally the *SILL — socle interministériel des logiciels
libres.* A P1 officer understands "socle" instantly: the trusted technical
foundation everything else stands on.
- **Pronunciation:** SOK-luh — clean.
- **Collision — the catch:** it is *so* native it is nearly generic and hard to
  own as a mark; the SILL association is both instant credibility (+) and weak
  distinctiveness (−). Semantically it says *foundation*, not *provenance* —
  slightly off the money thesis.
- **Use it if** you want zero pronunciation risk and maximum procurement
  familiarity, and are willing to trade trademark strength for it.
- Scores: C1 4.5 · C2 3 · C3 4.5 · C4 2.5 · C5 5 · C6 3 · C7 3.5.

### 9. **Provance** — coined, provenance-rooted — **73/100**
The representative *coinage* lane (the "or whatever we call it" slot from the
positioning doc). Compresses *provenance*; warmly echoes *Provence* (a friendly
EU-territorial association). Ownable and on-theme.
- **Pronunciation:** pro-VANSS — clean.
- **Collision:** scattered minor usage; the `-ance` ending drifts slightly
  startup-ward, which costs it with P1/P3.
- Scores: C1 3.5 · C2 4.5 · C3 4 · C4 3 · C5 3.5 · C6 2.5 · C7 4.

### 10. **Repère** — the datum / benchmark — **72/100**
Surveying/cartography register: a *repère* is a fixed known reference point you
measure everything from (*point de repère*). Nice ground-truth fit, and it is a
near-homophone of *repaire* — a fox's **den/lair** — a quiet two-brand bridge to
`vix` that reads externally as serious datum vocabulary.
- **Pronunciation:** ruh-PAIR — the accents are a mild spelling tax.
- **Collision:** common word; moderate ownability. The pun risk (den/datum) can
  undercut gravitas if leaned on.
- Scores: C1 3.5 · C2 3.5 · C3 4 · C4 3 · C5 4 · C6 4.5 · C7 3.

### 11. **Sillage** — the wake / trail left behind — **71/100**
*Sillage* is the trail a ship leaves — provenance as the visible track behind
the artifact. Evocative, distinctly French.
- **Pronunciation:** see-YAHZH — hard for non-French speakers; strong perfumery
  connotation (a fragrance's *sillage*) dilutes the institutional read.
- **Collision:** moderate.
- Scores: C1 3.5 · C2 4 · C3 3 · C4 3.5 · C5 4 · C6 3 · C7 3.5.

### 12. **Filière** — the supply-chain thread — **64/100** *(instructive reject)*
Included as the cautionary entry. *La filière* is exactly the procurement /
industrial-policy word for a supply chain — P1 says it daily. That is the
problem: it is a **generic common noun**, un-ownable as a mark, and it names the
*category* rather than *your product*. A perfect vocabulary match that fails the
trademark test teaches the boundary: on-thesis meaning is necessary but not
sufficient — you must be able to *own* the word.
- Scores: C1 3.5 · C2 3.5 · C3 4 · **C4 1** · C5 4.5 · C6 2.5 · C7 3.

## (c) Recommendation: **Vidimus**

It is the only candidate that scores at the top of *every* heavily-weighted
criterion at once. It carries the money thesis in its literal meaning — *"we
have seen; here is the certified true record"* is the definition of an audit and
the definition of a vixen receipt in the same breath. It is trademark-clean and
ownable where the more obviously-meaningful candidates (Filigrane, Filière) are
not. It is dictatable and pronounceable in every EU language where the
most-French candidates (Poinçon, Sillage) tax the pan-European buyer. And it is
the structural anti-Gaia-X: a dry, clerical, evidentiary Latin word — a notary's
attestation, not a goddess or a federation — which is exactly the register a
scarred sovereignty evaluator is looking to be reassured by. It sits cleanly
beside the language: **`vix` builds; every artifact ships its *vidimus*.**

The one cost — no instant meaning to a layperson — is the same "cost" Atos,
Idemia, Thales and Veritas all pay by design, and it converts into a story
asset the moment anyone asks.

**The paragraph a procurement document could carry:**

> **Vidimus** is a European build and software-supply-chain assurance platform.
> A *vidimus* — Latin, "we have seen" — was historically a sealed, certified
> true copy: an official attestation that a record faithfully matches its
> authentic original. Vidimus produces exactly this for software. Every artifact
> it builds carries a machine-verified record of its complete provenance — every
> input, every tool, identified by content hash, exhaustive by construction
> rather than by declaration — together with an embedded, signed recipe that lets
> any auditor reproduce and re-verify the artifact from the binary alone.
> Vulnerability advisories are matched against the actual code an organisation
> ships, not against version ranges, enabling exact fleet-wide answers to "is the
> affected code in anything we deploy?" Its registry and cache are operated
> within the European Union, by an EU company, on EU infrastructure. Vidimus
> converts CRA, NIS2 and DORA obligations from periodic paperwork into a
> structural property of the build itself: not a report about what a build did,
> but the build's own attested record of what it saw.

---

*Runner-up guidance:* if Amos wants richer instant meaning for a
French-ministry-first launch and will accept the pronunciation tax, **Poinçon**
is the fallback — the assay-hallmark story is the single most vivid articulation
of the thesis. If Amos wants the *safest* possible procurement familiarity with
zero pronunciation risk, **Socle** is the conservative pick, at the cost of
trademark strength. The recommendation stands at **Vidimus**.
