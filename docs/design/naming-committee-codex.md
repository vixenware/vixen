# Naming committee: institutional product

Seat: Codex, independent pass. Context read: `docs/design/eu-sovereignty-positioning.md`
and `docs/content/provenance.md`.

Assumption: the language keeps the vix identity and the fox. This memo names the
institutional product: the thing written into a ministry tender, defended by a
CISO, or scored by a sovereignty fund. Quick collision notes are public-web
sanity checks, not EUIPO, WIPO, domain, or legal clearance.

Public-web collision anchors checked: French official cadastre services
([cadastre.gouv.fr](https://www.cadastre.gouv.fr/),
[cadastre.data.gouv.fr](https://cadastre.data.gouv.fr/),

## Persona weighting

1. French public-procurement officer, 40%
   - Needs a name that can be typed into a CCTP/RC/AAP without sounding like a
     consumer startup.
   - Rewards administrative vocabulary, auditability, and neutral public-interest
     posture.
   - Penalizes cute animal carryover, English-only swagger, impossible spelling,
     crypto-ledger language, and names that sound like certifications we do not
     yet hold.

2. CISO at an EU critical-sector company, 35%
   - Needs a name they can put in a risk memo, board slide, and CRA/NIS2/DORA
     remediation plan.
   - Rewards words that imply evidence, inventory, chain of custody, and
     operational defensibility.
   - Penalizes grand sovereignty theater, vague trust claims, collision with
     existing security vendors, and names that overpromise certification rather
     than mechanism.

3. Sovereignty-fund evaluator, 25%
   - Needs a name that can score as infrastructure, not another Gaia-X-shaped
     promise layer.
   - Rewards European-language portability, public-good undertone, and a clear
     link from name to product mechanism: observed reads, receipts, content
     identity, embedded recipes, EU-operated registry/cache.
   - Penalizes "platform of platforms" abstraction, pan-European slogans without
     product proof, and names that sound like tokenization, Web3, or ESG vapor.

## Recommendation

Use **Cadastre** as the institutional product name, preferably in an owned form:
**Vixenware Cadastre** for formal procurement and **Cadastre by vixenware** for
website/product copy.

Cadastre is the only direction that is simultaneously French-procurement-native,
EU-portable, non-cute, non-crypto, and mechanically faithful to the product. It
does not merely say "proof"; it names an official map of bounded assets and
their rights, restrictions, and responsibilities. That metaphor lands cleanly:
software artifacts become parcels; receipts are the surveyed record; content
hashes define boundaries; embedded recipes describe how the parcel was produced;
advisory matching answers which parcels contain vulnerable material. The name
also avoids Gaia-X-style abstraction because a cadastre is not a slogan. It is a
working register.

## Procurement paragraph

**Vixenware Cadastre** is a sovereign software-supply-chain cadastre operated by
an EU company for regulated software estates. It records each build artifact by
content identity and attaches the build's observed read-set receipt, toolchain,
and recipe, so CRA, NIS2, and DORA evidence is produced by the build mechanism
itself rather than by detached CI paperwork. Deployed artifacts can be audited in
place, rebuilt from their recorded recipe, and checked fleet-wide against
content-keyed advisories, with registry and cache operation designed for EU
jurisdiction and auditability.

## Top three

1. **Cadastre** - recommended. Most credible across procurement, CISO, and
   sovereignty-funder evaluation.
2. **Probatio** - strong evidence semantics, but less concrete and more
   collision-prone.
3. **Registre** - excellent French administrative descriptor, weaker as a
   standalone mark; useful as supporting vocabulary even if not chosen as the
   product name.
